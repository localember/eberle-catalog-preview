The short answer

Sciatica is pain that travels along the sciatic nerve, from the low back through the buttock and down the leg. Chiropractic care works on what is irritating the nerve, easing pressure on the joints and tissue around it.

Sciatica is the one people describe with their hands, drawing a line down the back of the leg. It is also the one that frightens people most, because a nerve makes itself known in a way a stiff joint does not. Here is a plain-language look at what causes it, how chiropractic care can help, and when it is something else.

What causes sciatica?

The sciatic nerve is the longest in your body, and it runs from the low back through the buttock and down the leg. Sciatica is what you feel when something along that path irritates or compresses it. Most often the source is mechanical: a disc that is pressing where it should not, a joint that has stiffened and narrowed the space the nerve travels through, or muscle in the buttock clamping down around it.

The usual contributors we see in Dripping Springs:

  • A disc bulge or herniation in the low back pressing on the nerve root.
  • Prolonged sitting, which loads the low back and compresses the path the nerve takes.
  • A single awkward lift or twist that changed something and has not settled.
  • Tight, guarded muscle through the buttock and hip that squeezes the nerve as it passes.
  • Age-related narrowing that leaves the nerve less room than it used to have.

Some leg pain is not sciatica, and some sciatica needs a medical work-up first. Pain down both legs, weakness that is getting worse, or any change in bladder or bowel control is a medical emergency and needs to be seen now, not booked. Part of a good first visit is ruling that out.

How does chiropractic care help with sciatica?

Chiropractic care works on the mechanics of what is crowding the nerve. Gentle, specific adjustments restore movement to joints that have stiffened and guarded, which can take pressure off the irritated tissue around the nerve root. Alongside the hands-on work, you get movement and position guidance so the leg settles and stays settled between visits.

In practice, a course of care usually blends a few things:

  • Adjustments to restore motion in the low back and pelvis, matched in force to a nerve that is already irritable.
  • Soft-tissue work through the buttock and hip, where the nerve is often squeezed on its way past.
  • Positions and movements that help the pain move out of your leg.
  • Practical changes to sitting, driving, and lifting, so the same pressure does not keep rebuilding.

The first goal is to settle this episode, then give you the movement and habits to keep the leg out of it.

Sciatica questions we hear most

Is chiropractic care safe for sciatica?

For most people with common, mechanical sciatica, gentle chiropractic care has a strong safety record. An irritated nerve deserves a lighter hand, so the first visit is a careful history and check, and low-force options are used while the leg is angry. If your history or exam points to something that should be seen medically first, we will tell you.

How long until my leg settles?

Many people notice the leg pain pulling back toward the back within the first couple of weeks, which is usually the first sign of progress even before the pain drops. It depends on what is irritating the nerve and how long it has been going on. After your first visit we will give you a realistic window rather than a guess.

Do I need an MRI first?

Not usually. Most mechanical sciatica does not need imaging to begin care, and a thorough history and physical check is the standard starting point. If your exam shows progressive weakness, or your history points to something that warrants imaging, we will arrange or refer for it before going further.

Should I rest or keep moving?

Keep moving, gently, within what the leg allows. Long stretches of bed rest tend to stiffen things and slow recovery for most people. Short walks and frequent position changes usually beat staying still. What we will do is give you the specific movements that suit your pattern, because the wrong ones can wind an irritated nerve up.

When is sciatica an emergency?

Pain down both legs at once, weakness that is getting worse rather than better, numbness through the saddle area, or any change in bladder or bowel control needs urgent medical attention the same day. Those are rare, and they are not something to bring to a chiropractor first. Everything short of that is worth a conversation.
